Health Insurance Terms Explained: Premium, Deductible, and Copay

By Kang Ruli · Updated 2026

Note: This article is general education, not insurance or financial advice. Confirm details in your own policy document and speak with a licensed advisor before buying or changing coverage.

Health insurance documents use many specialized terms. Understanding a few core words helps you compare plans and estimate costs more accurately.

Premium

The premium is the amount you pay for coverage, usually monthly. Paying the premium does not mean the insurer pays every bill; that depends on the other terms.

Deductible

The deductible is the amount you pay for covered care before the insurer begins sharing costs, typically within a plan year. Some services may be covered before the deductible is met, so check the plan's list.

Copay and coinsurance

A copay is a fixed amount for a service, such as a doctor visit. Coinsurance is a percentage of the cost you share after the deductible.

Out-of-pocket maximum

This is the most you pay for covered care in a plan year. After you reach it, covered services are generally paid in full by the insurer, though rules vary by plan.

How to use these terms

Compare plans by estimating your likely yearly costs: premiums plus expected deductible and copays. A plan with a lower premium is not always cheaper for someone who needs frequent care.

Frequently asked questions

Is a lower premium always better?

No. A lower premium often comes with a higher deductible or out-of-pocket maximum, so the total yearly cost depends on your health needs.
