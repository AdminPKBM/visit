A Beginner's Home Studio for Recording Video Courses

By Kang Ruli · Updated 2026

You do not need an expensive studio to record a clear online course. Good light and clean audio matter far more than camera brand. A small, well-planned setup can produce professional-looking lessons.

Lighting

Place your main light in front of you, slightly to one side, and use a window or softbox as the main source. Avoid lights behind you, which turn your face into a silhouette.

Audio

Audio problems cause more student complaints than video problems. A lavalier microphone clipped near your chest gives clearer sound than a laptop microphone. Record in a room with soft furniture, curtains, or a rug to reduce echo.

Camera and framing

Your phone camera is often enough. Set it at eye level, keep the frame steady on a tripod, and leave a little space above your head. Record in one resolution throughout the course.

Editing workflow

Record each lesson in short segments, mark mistakes as you go, and cut them later. Keep a consistent intro and outro so the course feels cohesive.

Equipment prices vary widely, so compare options when you are ready to buy.

Frequently asked questions

Should I record in one take?

Short segments are easier to re-record and edit. Long takes increase the chance of fatigue and repeated mistakes.
