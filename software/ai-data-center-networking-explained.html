How AI Data Centers Move Data: A Plain-Language Guide to Optical Networking

By Kang Ruli · Updated 2026

Training and running large AI models depends on moving enormous amounts of data quickly between processors. Much of that movement now happens over optical links, which send data as pulses of light through fiber. Understanding why helps you read industry news without being swayed by headlines.

Why light instead of copper

Copper cables are cheap and work well over short distances, but their signal weakens quickly as they get longer and as speeds rise. Optical links carry more data over longer distances and generally use less energy per bit at high speeds. As AI clusters grow, the power and space used by the connections themselves become a real design constraint.

Three layers of connectivity

  • Inside a cluster. Processors inside a rack or across a set of racks need very short, very fast links. Here, copper and short-reach optics both play roles.
  • Between buildings. Companies often spread AI workloads across several data centers on one campus or nearby sites. These links need more reach and more capacity.
  • Long distance. Metro, regional, long-haul, and undersea networks carry traffic between cities and countries. These networks have existed for decades, and AI adds demand for more capacity on top of them.

Terms worth knowing

A transceiver converts electrical signals into light and back again. Bandwidth describes how much data a link can carry per second. Latency is the delay before data arrives, which matters for tightly coupled computing. Power per bit measures how much energy each unit of data costs to move, a figure that increasingly shapes purchasing decisions.

How to read industry news critically

Articles about this sector often cite large market sizes and forecasts. Before accepting them, check three things. First, find the original source of the estimate and its date. Second, notice whether a figure describes total market size or one company's revenue. Third, look for the assumptions behind the growth rate, since small changes in those assumptions can produce very different results.

This article is educational and does not offer investment advice. Market forecasts are estimates and can change quickly.

Frequently asked questions

Do all AI data centers use optical links?

Not every connection does, but optical links are common wherever distance, bandwidth, or power efficiency becomes important.

Why does this matter for software teams?

Network capacity and latency affect how fast AI applications respond and how much they cost to run, which in turn shapes the pricing of cloud services.
